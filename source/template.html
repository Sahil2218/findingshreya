I · Before

For a while there, I was just getting through the days.

I don't think I noticed how heavy it had gotten. You carry something long enough and it stops feeling like weight — it just becomes the weather. I wasn't unhappy exactly. I was grey. Nothing in me was growing.

II · The garden

My life was a green garden with dead flowers in it.

Then she turned up, and they opened again. Same garden. Same soil. Every single thing in it suddenly alive.

Nothing about the garden changed. Only that she was in it.

III · The fire

Now when I picture peace, it’s a beach at dusk.

The tide doing its slow thing. Driftwood stacked into a pile. That first proper catch of the flame — and then the whole evening in front of us, with absolutely nothing either of us has to do.

IV · The cooking

And we’d cook right there beside it.

Nothing fancy. Something warm in a battered pot, made slowly because there’s no reason on earth to hurry. Eaten close enough to the fire that you keep having to shift back from the heat.

V · The camp

A small camp, and the sea somewhere out in the dark.

You hear it more than you see it. The fire drops down to embers. She’s right there, and there is nowhere else that either of us is supposed to be.

VI · The sky

And then the whole thing opens up above us.

That’s the part I keep coming back to. Lying flat on the sand next to her, saying almost nothing, with more stars overhead than the two of us know what to do with.

VII · Now

This is what peace looks like to me now.

It looks like her.
